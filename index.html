About

I am a Research Fellow at the Australian National University (ANU) School of Computing (2025–), working with Prof. Alex Potanin on formal verification for secure embedded systems. My research sits at the intersection of formal methods, programming languages, and systems security, with a long-term goal of making formal verification a natural part of the software development lifecycle — specifically, automating the formalisation process to close the gap between verification’s clear value and its steep adoption barrier.

I hold a PhD in Computer Science from the National University of Singapore (2014), where I was supervised by Prof. Jin Song Dong. My thesis applied model checking to pervasive computing systems.

My background spans academia and industry across four countries. Before joining ANU I was:

  • Staff Engineer, Pi Squared Inc. (2024–2025)
    Zero-knowledge circuits and cross-chain protocols
  • Rust Security Lead, Runtime Verification (2022–2024)
    Rust MIR semantics in the K Framework; leading smart contract audits
  • Individual Contributor, AntGroup (2019–2022)
  • Sessional Lecturer, Monash University (2018–2019)
  • Research Fellow, NUS / ADSC–UIUC (2013–2017)

Projects

My current work centres on continuous verification while developing embedded systems on the seL4 microkernel.

  • Fiducia — Secure and Deadlock-Free by Construction
    A language and compiler for applications on the formally verified seL4 microkernel. Each protection domain is a CSP-style process orchestrating channel communication and computation in C or Pancake; a two-level information-flow type system keeps sensitive data from leaking, and every well-formed system is deadlock-free and deterministic. Every build is also a verification run: the compiler auto-formalises a system model from the Fiducia programs, with the C/Pancake code’s contracts (proved by Frama-C or Viper) as event specifications, so the model never drifts from the code that ships. The core language’s semantics are mechanised in Lean 4. Joint work with Prof. Alex Potanin (ANU) and collaborators at UNSW.

Past Projects

  • mir-semantics — Formal Semantics of Rust MIR in K
    Formal operational semantics of Rust’s Middle Intermediate Representation (MIR) in the K Framework, enabling concrete and symbolic execution of Rust programs, step-by-step state inspection, and a foundation for full formal verification. Ships as kmir, a Python front-end to the semantics. Developed at Runtime Verification (2022–2024).
